HIST 1020 - WWI lecture notes (weeks 5-6)

Long-term causes (MAIN)
- Militarism: European powers built up huge armies and navies. Britain and Germany were locked in a naval arms race over battleships called dreadnoughts.
- Alliances: Europe split into two blocs. The Triple Entente was France, Russia, and Britain. The Triple Alliance was Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy.
- Imperialism: competition for colonies in Africa and Asia raised tensions between the powers.
- Nationalism: Slavic nationalism in the Balkans threatened Austria-Hungary, which ruled many Slavic peoples.

The spark
On June 28, 1914, Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, was assassinated in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip. Princip was a Bosnian Serb linked to the Black Hand, a Serbian nationalist group. Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia one month later, and the alliance system pulled the other powers in within weeks.

Western Front
Germany followed the Schlieffen Plan, which aimed to defeat France quickly by marching through neutral Belgium before turning to fight Russia. The invasion of Belgium brought Britain into the war. The plan failed at the First Battle of the Marne in September 1914, when French and British troops stopped the German advance near Paris. After the Marne, both sides dug trenches, and the Western Front became a stalemate stretching from the North Sea to Switzerland.
- Trench warfare: soldiers lived in muddy trenches separated by no man's land.
- Battle of Verdun (1916): the longest battle of the war, fought between France and Germany.
- Battle of the Somme (1916): on the first day alone, Britain suffered about 57,000 casualties.

New technology
Machine guns made frontal attacks across open ground extremely deadly. Poison gas, first used on a large scale by Germany at Ypres in 1915, caused terror but rarely decided battles. Tanks were first used by the British at the Somme in 1916. Airplanes were used at first mainly for scouting enemy positions.

The US enters
The US stayed neutral at first. Germany's policy of unrestricted submarine warfare, which sank ships without warning, angered Americans, especially after the sinking of the Lusitania in 1915. The Zimmermann Telegram (1917) was a secret German message proposing an alliance with Mexico against the US. The US declared war on Germany in April 1917.

Russia leaves the war
After the Bolshevik Revolution in 1917, Russia signed the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk with Germany in March 1918 and left the war.

End of the war
The armistice took effect on November 11, 1918. The Treaty of Versailles (1919) forced Germany to accept blame for the war in the "war guilt clause," pay reparations, give up its colonies, and limit its army to 100,000 men. Prof said: the harsh terms created resentment in Germany that later helped the rise of the Nazis.
